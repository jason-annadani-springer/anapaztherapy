Understanding CBT

What is Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)?

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) is a practical and supportive form of talking therapy that helps you understand the connection between your thoughts, feelings and behaviours, and how these can influence one another. When we are struggling with anxiety, low mood, stress, or the effects of difficult or traumatic experiences, it can be easy to fall into patterns of thinking and responding that leave us feeling stuck.

CBT offers a safe space to explore these patterns with curiosity and without judgement. Together, we can begin to understand where they may have come from and find more helpful ways of responding. CBT is collaborative and tailored to you. We will work together to understand what you are experiencing, identify what you would like to change, and develop practical strategies that feel manageable and relevant to your everyday life. This can include learning to challenge unhelpful thoughts, develop greater self-compassion and respond to difficult emotions in healthier ways.

The aim is not to simply manage symptoms, but to help you develop a better understanding of yourself and feel more able to navigate life's challenges with greater confidence and emotional wellbeing.

What can CBT help with?

CBT can be helpful for a variety of concerns. In my practise, my main areas of interest are anxiety and trauma, and I work with people experiencing a range of difficulties, including:

  • Anxiety, excessive worry and feeling overwhelmed
  • Social anxiety and health anxiety
  • The effects of trauma and difficult or distressing experiences
  • Low mood and depression
  • Obsessive Compulsive behaviours, including intrusive thoughts and excessive checking
What to expect at APT?

Free 30-minute introductory session
Before beginning therapy, I offer a free 30-minute introductory session. This is an opportunity for us to meet, briefly discuss what has brought you to seek therapy, and consider whether working together feels right for you. You will also have the opportunity to ask questions about the therapeutic process and how I work. There is no obligation to proceed with therapy following this meeting.

Your first therapy session
The first full therapy session provides an opportunity to explore in greater depth what has brought you to therapy, how your difficulties are affecting your life, and what you would like to change. We will also begin to explore relevant personal history and circumstances to develop a clearer understanding of your difficulties and establish a shared direction for our work together.

Assessment and treatment planning
Together, we will identify your therapeutic goals and agree on a treatment plan tailored to your individual needs, circumstances and priorities. This plan will help guide our work together and may be reviewed as therapy progresses.

Session length
Sessions typically last 50–60 minutes, depending on the agreed arrangements and, where applicable, the requirements of your insurance provider.

Frequency and duration
The frequency and number of sessions will depend on your individual circumstances, needs and therapeutic goals. Progress will be reviewed throughout therapy, allowing us to consider what is working well and whether any adjustments may be helpful.

Working collaboratively
Therapy is a collaborative process in which your experiences, perspectives and priorities are central to our work together. You will be encouraged to take an active role in therapy and may be offered practical strategies, exercises or activities to practise between sessions. These can help you apply what you learn in therapy to everyday situations and gradually develop skills to manage difficulties more independently.

Further information

For more information about cognitive behaviour therapy please see the website of the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies https://babcp.com/about/who-are-babcp/
