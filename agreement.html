Our agreement

Last updated: 11/2026

Therapy is a collaborative process based on mutual respect, shared responsibilities and clear expectations. Before therapy begins, you will be invited to read and sign a therapeutic agreement outlining the terms of our work together and providing an opportunity to discuss any questions or concerns.
My aim is to provide a safe, confidential and supportive space where you can explore your thoughts, feelings and difficulties without judgement. This section explains the key aspects of our work together, including confidentiality, safety, professional boundaries, supervision, appointments and cancellations, and how to raise any questions or concerns about my practice.

CBT is generally suited to short- to medium-term therapy, although everyone's needs and circumstances are different. If a different or longer-term approach appears more appropriate, we will discuss this together and consider the available options. Where appropriate, I can help you identify alternative approaches or sources of further therapeutic support.

Confidentiality and sharing of information

Confidentiality is an essential part of our therapeutic relationship. Information you share during sessions will be treated with respect and kept confidential, subject to certain legal, ethical and safeguarding obligations.
There may be circumstances in which I need to share information without your consent. These may include, but are not limited to, the following:

  • Risk of harm to yourself: Where I have serious concerns about your safety, including the risk of suicide or significant self-harm, I may need to contact your GP or another appropriate service to help keep you safe.
  • Risk of harm to others: Where I have reason to believe that you may pose a serious risk of harm to another person or cause significant damage to their property, I may need to take appropriate action to protect those affected.
  • Safeguarding concerns: Where there are safeguarding concerns involving a child or an adult at risk.
  • Legal requirements: Where disclosure is required by law, such as in response to a valid court order.
  • Professional and ethical responsibilities: Where sharing information is otherwise legally justified or required by my professional and ethical responsibilities.
  • Sharing with healthcare professionals: With a GP or another healthcare professional, where sharing is otherwise lawful and justified, even if your consent has not been obtained.

Wherever possible and appropriate, I will discuss any proposed disclosure with you beforehand and explain the reasons for it, unless doing so could increase the risk of harm or compromise a necessary safeguarding or legal response.
In these circumstances, I will consider the situation carefully and share only information that is necessary and proportionate.
I treat your personal information as confidential and do not sell it or share it for marketing purposes. Wherever information is shared, I take care to disclose only what is necessary and proportionate for the relevant purpose.
Further information about how your personal information is collected, used, stored, protected and retained is provided in the APT Privacy and Recordkeeping Policy.

Appointment and cancellations

  • Please provide at least 24 hours' notice if you are unable to attend an appointment.
  • The full session fee will be charged for missed appointments or appointments cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice.
  • Late arrival: Sessions will normally finish at the scheduled time and cannot usually be extended if you arrive late. The therapist will normally wait for up to 30 minutes from the scheduled start time.
  • If there are repeated missed appointments, cancellations or outstanding payments, this will be discussed with you and may result in a review of the therapy agreement.
  • Holding appointments: Once an appointment has been offered to a new or existing client, it will be held for 24 hours. After this time, the appointment may be released and offered to another client.
  • Alternative appointments: Every effort will be made to offer an alternative appointment where possible. However, availability cannot always be guaranteed, and you may need to join a waiting list for a suitable appointment time.

Professional supervision and consultation

Professional supervision or clinical consultation is an important part of maintaining safe, ethical and effective therapeutic practice. I may discuss aspects of my clinical work with my supervisor or consultant to reflect on the therapeutic process, consider ethical issues and support the quality of the service I provide.

I take care to protect your confidentiality during supervision and consultation. Wherever possible, I avoid sharing information that could identify you. Where identifiable information needs to be discussed, I will handle it in accordance with my professional and ethical obligations and applicable confidentiality and data protection requirements.

Alcohol and recreational drugs

To support a safe and productive therapeutic space, you are expected to attend sessions free from the effects of alcohol or recreational drugs.
If you attend a session under their influence, I may need to postpone or cancel the session if I consider that meaningful or safe therapeutic work is not possible. Repeated occurrences may lead to a review of our therapeutic agreement and could result in the ending of our work together.

Respectful behaviour and professional boundaries

A safe, respectful and professional relationship is essential to effective therapy. I am committed to providing a space in which you can speak openly and explore difficult experiences without judgement.

Verbal or physical aggression, threatening behaviour or other conduct that compromises safety will be taken seriously. In such circumstances, I may need to review our therapeutic agreement, take appropriate steps to maintain safety, or consider whether our sessions can continue.

If it becomes necessary to end our work together, I will aim to approach this thoughtfully and discuss the decision with you wherever possible, including appropriate next steps or alternative sources of support where suitable.

Questions or concerns

If you have questions or concerns about confidentiality, professional boundaries or how I respond to issues of safety, you are welcome to raise these with me directly. As an independent practitioner registered with the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP), you may also contact BABCP if you wish to seek guidance or raise a concern about my professional practice. Further information is available at www.babcp.com.

For further information about how your personal information is handled, please refer to the Privacy Policy section.

If you need urgent support

APT is not a crisis or emergency service. If you are currently in crisis or experiencing thoughts of suicide, please reach out for immediate support from:

The Samaritans, who are available 24/7, free of charge, on 116 123; or

if you are in immediate danger, please call 999 or go to your nearest A&E.
