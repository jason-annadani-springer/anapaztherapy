About me

Ana Paz, MSc CBT | BABCP Accredited CBT Therapist | DipSW


I am a British-Spanish CBT Therapist with a Master’s degree in Cognitive Behavioural Therapy from Buckinghamshire New University, England, and a Diploma in Social Work from the University of Santiago de Compostela, Spain. I am an accredited member of the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP, Member #).

I offer personalised CBT for adults, with a particular focus on trauma and anxiety, while also supporting a wide range of other challenges that may be affecting your wellbeing.

My professional background


I am a compassionate therapist with over 20 years of experience supporting people through a wide range of life’s challenges. I was born in London, grew up and studied in Spain, and later returned to the UK to continue my professional journey. I began my career as a social worker before specialising in Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT).

This combination of experience has given me a broad understanding of how the pressures and challenges of everyday life, including family, work, health and housing, can affect our emotional wellbeing.

Throughout my career, I have worked with adults and children across the NHS and local authorities, supporting people with anxiety, low mood, stress, trauma and difficult life changes. I have also had the opportunity to work with people from a wide range of cultural backgrounds, which has deepened my understanding of how culture and individual experiences can shape our wellbeing. Today, my therapy work is focused on supporting adults. I understand that everyone’s circumstances are different, so I take a practical, collaborative and culturally sensitive approach to therapy.

In our sessions, we will work together to understand what you are going through, identify what you would like to change, and develop practical strategies to help you feel more able to cope. I use evidence-based CBT techniques and will support you to build confidence and skills that you can continue to use in everyday life.

I have a particular interest in working with adults who have experienced trauma, helping them understand how past experiences may continue to affect their thoughts, emotions and relationships, and supporting them to move towards greater understanding, confidence and wellbeing.

People I work with often describe me as warm, approachable and supportive. I aim to create a safe and understanding space where you can talk openly, without judgement, while also helping you take manageable steps towards the changes that are important to you.

Whether you are looking for support with a current difficulty, want to develop practical coping skills, or are hoping to make longer-term changes, I offer a calm, down-to-earth space where you can feel supported and understood.

As a native Spanish speaker, I can also offer therapy sessions in Spanish.

Why I chose to be a therapist


For many years, I experienced anxiety myself, and I became a therapist because therapy changed the way I understood myself. I began to recognise that many of the thoughts, emotions, and behaviours I had struggled with were not signs that something was wrong with me, but ways my mind and body had learned to protect me from perceived threats. This helped me become less critical of myself, develop greater self-compassion, and experience life more fully. I also discovered how CBT could offer practical tools to help me make sense of these experiences and navigate them differently.

This experience has shaped the way I see and understand other people’s struggles too. I believe that behind anxiety, low mood, life difficulties, and the effects of trauma, there is often a person doing their very best to cope with what they have experienced and find their way through life.

My goal is to help you make sense of your experiences, reconnect with your strengths and capabilities, and develop a kinder relationship with yourself. I aim to provide a safe and supportive space where you can explore what you are going through without judgement or shame and find practical ways to move forward with greater self-compassion.

If you need urgent support

APT is not a crisis or emergency service. If you’re currently in crisis or experiencing thoughts of suicide, please reach out for immediate support.

Samaritans are available 24/7, free of charge, on 116 123.

If you are in immediate danger, please call 999 or go to your nearest A&E.
